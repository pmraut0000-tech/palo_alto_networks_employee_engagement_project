# Diagnostic Summary

## Important Data Note
This is an **illustrative academic/portfolio analysis** based on a synthetic dataset. It is not an assessment of actual Palo Alto Networks employees and should not be interpreted as evidence about the company's real workforce.

## Executive Summary
The synthetic dataset contains **500 employees**.

Average scores:
- Engagement: **3.01 / 5**
- Satisfaction: **3.03 / 5**
- Burnout: **2.72 / 5**
- Intent to Stay: **3.55 / 5**

Employees classified as high burnout risk: **18.8%**.

In this synthetic sample:
- The department with the highest average burnout is **Finance**.
- The department with the lowest average engagement is **IT**.

## Diagnostic Themes

### 1. Engagement
Engagement should be interpreted alongside manager support, recognition, career growth, psychological safety, and work-life balance. Strong scores on these dimensions generally support higher engagement.

### 2. Satisfaction
Satisfaction is influenced by both intrinsic and extrinsic factors, especially career growth, work-life balance, compensation satisfaction, and manager support.

### 3. Burnout
Burnout risk should be monitored using workload, weekly working hours, work-life balance, support, absence behavior, and psychological safety.

### 4. Retention
Intent to stay is treated as an early retention indicator. Employees with lower engagement and satisfaction or higher burnout may require targeted interventions.

## Recommended HR Actions
1. Establish department-level burnout monitoring.
2. Review workload and staffing where weekly hours remain consistently high.
3. Strengthen manager coaching and regular check-ins.
4. Improve recognition practices and career-development visibility.
5. Monitor psychological safety through pulse surveys.
6. Use quarterly engagement and burnout dashboards.
7. Segment interventions by department, role level, tenure, and work mode.

## Suggested KPIs
- Engagement index
- Satisfaction index
- Burnout-risk rate
- Intent-to-stay score
- Average weekly hours
- Manager-support score
- Career-growth score
- Recognition score
- Psychological-safety score
- Absence days

## Analytical Caution
Correlation does not prove causation. A real organizational diagnosis would require validated survey instruments, longitudinal data, confidentiality controls, and appropriate statistical testing.
